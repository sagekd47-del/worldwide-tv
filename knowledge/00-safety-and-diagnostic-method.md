# General electronics safety and evidence-based diagnosis

## Diagnostic method
1. Record the exact device category, manufacturer, complete model number, operating system or firmware version, and exact error text/code.
2. Establish when the fault began, what changed immediately beforehand (update, drop, liquid exposure, new cable, new app, power outage), whether it is repeatable, and what has already been tried.
3. Reproduce only when safe. Change one variable at a time and record the result. Prefer reversible checks before resets or repairs.
4. Distinguish an observed symptom from a suspected cause. A symptom alone rarely proves a failed component.
5. Check the exact model's official manual/service information before giving model-specific button sequences, voltage values, firmware files, disassembly steps, or reset procedures.
6. Before factory reset, firmware update, disk repair, or reinstall, explain data-loss risk and advise a verified backup.

## Stop-use warning signs
Stop using and disconnect power only if it is safe to do so when there is smoke, sparks, crackling, burning smell, liquid inside, electric shock/tingle, severe overheating, a swollen/leaking battery, or damaged mains wiring. Do not charge a swollen or damaged battery. Do not open power supplies, CRT televisions, microwave ovens, or other high-voltage equipment. Refer internal electrical repair to a qualified technician.

## Evidence and uncertainty
Never invent a diagnosis, error-code meaning, firmware compatibility, part number, or repair success rate. If the local evidence is missing or ambiguous, ask for the complete model and error message and say that the exact cause is not yet established.
